## Lab notes

**Name the situation**

- **Who they are (role, not name), what you have observed, and how long it has been happening.:** A colleague from another department is involved in supporting a product development project. Over the past few weeks, some requested information and updates have not been received within the expected timeframe. This has affected the progress of the feasibility assessment and the timing of key project decisions. The situation involves coordination between departments and follow-up on outstanding inputs.

**Make a diagnosis**

- **Your diagnosis, plus one sentence on why. Is your frustration with their behavior, or with a decision you made?:** My diagnosis is System because the project depends on inputs and decisions from other departments, which may be outside the colleague’s control. Before treating this as an individual performance issue, I need to identify the actual blockers and clarify which responsibilities I should address as the product leader.

**Write your opening line**

- **One next action I will take in the next two weeks is:** I will arrange a meeting with the colleague to review the outstanding inputs, clarify responsibilities and deadlines, identify any blockers, and agree on specific follow-up actions.
- **The first sentence of the conversation I need to have is:** In our recent work on the product development project, some requested inputs have not been received within the agreed timeframe, which has affected our progress and delayed key decisions. I’d like to understand what is getting in the way and agree on how we can move forward effectively.

**AI role-play**

- **After you step out: what did the role-play change about how you will open this conversation for real?:** The role-play helped me realize that I should open the conversation with facts and explain the impact on the project rather than making assumptions about my colleague’s performance. If the colleague becomes defensive, I will ask open-ended questions to understand the actual blockers before suggesting solutions. I will clarify ownership, agree on specific deadlines, and follow up on commitments while maintaining accountability and a constructive working relationship. Key learning: Diagnose the real problem before judging performance, and balance empathy with accountability.

**Show and swap your team charter**

- **Where does the charter leave room for interpretation that could cause a conflict?:** The charter leaves room for interpretation in three main areas: 1. **Ownership boundaries:** The boundary between Product and Growth is not fully settled, especially around onboarding and the first sleep session. This could lead to disagreements about who owns the user experience after account creation. 2. **Decision-making authority:** Some decisions require joint agreement, but the charter does not always clarify what happens when the named owners disagree or when a decision affects multiple metrics. 3. **Escalation and accountability:** The executive sponsor is not yet identified, and the decision log does not have a defined location or owner. This could delay decisions and make accountability unclear. To improve the charter, I would explicitly define the Product–Growth boundary, clarify decision rights for overlapping areas, and name the executive sponsor and decision-log owner.
