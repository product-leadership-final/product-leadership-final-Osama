# Individual Insights: Strategy Development Process

> Module 6 · Lead the Team That Builds with AI

## Friction points

> The hardest part was balancing the opportunity to enter the Saudi wholesale market at an attractive price with the need to prove demand and protect profitability. Initial wholesaler feedback supported the target price of SAR 200 per unit, excluding VAT, but interest alone was not enough to justify a large production commitment.
> 
> I worked through this by breaking the strategy into measurable outcomes, validating customer demand and unit economics, and proposing a 90-day validation phase before scaling production.

## Key learnings

> I will continue to connect product strategy to measurable business outcomes rather than focusing only on product features and launch activities.
> 
> I will also make trade-offs explicit, define clear success criteria, challenge assumptions with evidence, and align stakeholders before committing significant resources. I will use AI to accelerate research and analysis while keeping business judgment and final decisions with the responsible people.

## "Aha!" moment

> My biggest realization was that a promising product idea is not the same as a validated business opportunity.
> 
> Instead of asking, "How can we launch an economical instant water heater?", I learned to ask, "What must we prove before we invest in scaling this product?"
> 
> This changed my approach from focusing primarily on launching the product to building evidence that customers will buy it at the target price and that the business can deliver it profitably. The 90-day validation phase became a way to reduce uncertainty before making a larger commitment.
