Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Winning aspiration: Wholesale customers can offer a reliable, safe, and competitively priced instant water heater that meets end-user needs and delivers an attractive resale margin.
Where to play: Saudi Arabian wholesale customers targeting price-conscious consumers seeking reliable, safe, compact instant water heaters for residential use.
How to win: Leverage Saudi Ceramics’ trusted brand, reliable quality, and safety standards to offer a competitively priced instant water heater with flexible installation options and attractive wholesale margins.

The hard no

We will not launch at scale based on verbal interest alone, because validating confirmed wholesale demand and profitability protects our investment and keeps the strategy focused on sustainable growth.

Objective and key results

Validate strong wholesale market demand and achieve sustainable profitability for the economic instant water heater in Saudi Arabia.
Secure confirmed wholesale orders for at least 10,000 units within 90 days.
Achieve a wholesale selling price of SAR 200 per unit or higher, excluding VAT.
Achieve a gross profit margin of at least 40% per unit while meeting all quality and safety requirements.

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

NOW — Validate the Opportunity

Outcome: Confirm that the product can win in the wholesale market at a viable price and cost.

Validate wholesaler demand and willingness to buy at SAR 200 per unit, excluding VAT.

Confirm the estimated total unit cost of SAR 120 and target gross margin of 40%.

Validate product specifications, safety requirements, compliance, and installation needs.

Benchmark competitors and confirm the target customer's value expectations.

Success evidence: Documented customer feedback, confirmed purchase commitments, validated costs, and technical feasibility.

NEXT — Prove the Business Case

Outcome: Demonstrate that the product can achieve repeatable wholesale sales and acceptable economics.

Run a controlled pilot with selected wholesale customers.

Target 10,000 units within 90 days as a validation objective.

Track actual sales, realized selling price, unit cost, customer feedback, and product defects.

Review production readiness and supply-chain capacity.

Success evidence: Pilot sales results, verified unit economics, customer feedback, and a documented Go/No-Go recommendation.

LATER — Scale with Confidence

Outcome: Expand the product sustainably if the validation results support investment.

Prepare a phased production and market rollout plan.

Expand wholesale distribution based on demonstrated demand.

Monitor profitability, inventory turnover, product quality, warranty claims, and customer satisfaction.

Use sales and market data to guide future product improvements.

Trade-off memo

Validate demand through wholesale customer interviews and pilot commitments, targeting 10,000 units within 90 days and confirming acceptance of the SAR 200 wholesale price before VAT.
Validate the estimated total cost of SAR 120 per unit and confirm a commercially viable wholesale price of SAR 200 excluding VAT, with a target gross margin of at least 40%, including all relevant production and commercial costs.
Validate a safe, reliable and manufacturable 7.5–8.5 kW instant water heater with a compact design, flexible installation options and Saudi Ceramics quality standards, then confirm technical feasibility and readiness for a controlled pilot.
Do not commit to large-scale production or inventory until wholesale demand, price acceptance and pilot purchase commitments validate the business opportunity.
Do not add premium features, unnecessary design complexity or costly specifications that increase unit cost without demonstrated customer demand or measurable commercial value.
Do not reduce essential safety, regulatory compliance or product reliability to achieve the target price, and do not proceed with a business case that fails to meet the agreed cost and margin requirements.

Module 3
Team charter
What we own

What this team owns; The Product team owns the product strategy, customer and wholesale-market validation, target specifications, value proposition, pricing recommendation, business case, MVP scope, roadmap, and go/no-go recommendation for the economic instant water heater. We are accountable for validating demand for 10,000 units within 90 days, testing acceptance of a SAR 200 wholesale price excluding VAT, validating the estimated SAR 120 total unit cost and at least 40% gross margin, and confirming that the 7.5–8.5 kW product can be manufactured safely and reliably.
Out of scope; The Product team does not independently own factory capacity and production scheduling, detailed engineering approval, manufacturing execution, quality release, regulatory certification, or sales-team execution. We will not target unrelated customer segments, add costly features without evidence of customer willingness to pay, pursue the lowest price at the expense of value, or recommend mass production before demand, cost, safety, and manufacturing readiness are validated.
Joint calls across boundaries; A joint decision is required before: Changing product specifications, installation requirements, or safety-related features. Accepting a cost increase or price reduction that threatens the target gross margin. Committing to tooling, production capacity, or mass-production quantities before validation gates are met. Changing launch timing when technical readiness, regulatory compliance, or manufacturing capacity is at risk. Making customer or commercial commitments that conflict with approved specifications, cost assumptions, or production readiness. Product leads these discussions. R&D/Engineering, Factory, QA/Compliance, Sales/Marketing, and Finance provide approval or input within their respective responsibilities.

How we decide

Feature and scope calls; The Product Manager makes final recommendations and decides routine product scope, feature prioritization, and roadmap sequencing within the approved strategy, business case, and safety requirements. R&D/Engineering owns technical feasibility and engineering approval. QA/Compliance owns quality and compliance release within its authority. Factory leadership decides manufacturing feasibility and production scheduling. Sales/Marketing owns sales execution and customer feedback. Finance validates cost and margin assumptions. No commercial or product-scope decision can override mandatory safety or regulatory requirements.
Cross-team conflicts; The teams first attempt to resolve disagreements directly in a cross-functional working session within two business days of identifying the conflict. If unresolved, the Product Manager documents the issue, customer and business impact, available options, risks, and recommended decision, then escalates it to the relevant functional managers within the next business day. The functional managers aim to resolve the issue within three additional business days. If the conflict remains unresolved, or threatens a critical safety, compliance, financial, or launch milestone, it is escalated to the project executive sponsor or General Manager for a decision. Safety or regulatory concerns trigger an immediate hold on the affected decision or activity; they do not wait for the normal escalation timeline.
Escalations from outside the team; The designated executive sponsor or General Manager resolves unresolved cross-functional trade-offs involving strategic priorities, resources, budget, or launch timing. Functional authorities retain their respective approval responsibilities for engineering, manufacturing, quality, and compliance. Executive escalation does not authorize bypassing mandatory safety or regulatory requirements. All material decisions, owners, rationale, and deadlines are documented and communicated to the affected teams.

Module 5
Financial model
The business case

Economic Instant Water Heater — Final Business Case 1. Strategic Bet Approve a 90-day validation phase for an economical instant water heater targeting the Saudi wholesale market before committing to large-scale production. Our target wholesale price is SAR 200 per unit, excluding VAT, with an estimated total unit cost of SAR 120. This implies preliminary gross profit of SAR 80 per unit and a 40% gross margin, subject to cost verification. 2. CFO Stress Test Check 1 — Load-bearing assumption The most critical assumption is that wholesalers will commit to purchasing 10,000 units at SAR 200 per unit, excluding VAT, within 90 days. If demand is 30% below target, commitments would reach only 7,000 units, and the expected gross profit at the estimated unit economics would fall from SAR 800,000 to SAR 560,000. This is an illustrative scenario, not a sales forecast. Check 2 — Retention sensitivity Customer churn and LTV are not directly applicable to this product business case because it is based on unit sales rather than recurring subscriptions. The more relevant sensitivity is unit cost. If cost increases by 20%, from SAR 120 to SAR 144, gross profit falls from SAR 80 to SAR 56 per unit, and gross margin declines from 40% to 28%. Check 3 — Payback reality check A reliable payback period cannot yet be calculated because the validation budget, launch costs, and other investment costs have not been established. Before approving full-scale production, Finance should verify the complete cost model and assess the investment using contribution margin and total cash investment, not gross margin alone. Check 4 — Kill criterion The revised kill criterion specifies a metric, numerical threshold, timeline, and financial consequence: By the end of day 90, proceed to a request for scale-up approval only if we have documented wholesaler purchase commitments for at least 10,000 units at SAR 200 per unit, excluding VAT, and verified total unit cost is no higher than SAR 120. Manufacturing and regulatory readiness must also be confirmed. If any condition is unmet, withhold large-scale production funding. Reassess pricing, costs, or the product proposition before seeking a new approval. 3. Funding Verdict I would approve the 90-day validation phase, but not full-scale production, because demand and unit economics remain unverified. The one thing to fix before presenting: Establish a clear budget cap for the 90-day validation phase and confirm which costs are included in the SAR 120 unit-cost estimate. This will let Finance assess the downside exposure and calculate a credible investment payback.

Kill criterion

At the end of the 90-day validation phase, do not approve large-scale production unless: Actual wholesaler orders or documented purchase commitments validate demand at SAR 200 per unit, excluding VAT. Verified total unit cost is no higher than SAR 120, supporting the preliminary 40% gross margin. Manufacturing and regulatory readiness are confirmed. Financial consequence: If these conditions are not met, withhold the large-scale production investment. Rework the pricing, cost structure, or product proposition and require a new approval before proceeding.

Module 6

What I learned

Where the strategy was hardest to write, what you will keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was balancing the opportunity to enter the Saudi wholesale market at an attractive price with the need to prove demand and protect profitability. Initial wholesaler feedback supported the target price of SAR 200 per unit, excluding VAT, but interest alone was not enough to justify a large production commitment.

I worked through this by breaking the strategy into measurable outcomes, validating customer demand and unit economics, and proposing a 90-day validation phase before scaling production.

Key learnings

I will continue to connect product strategy to measurable business outcomes rather than focusing only on product features and launch activities.

I will also make trade-offs explicit, define clear success criteria, challenge assumptions with evidence, and align stakeholders before committing significant resources. I will use AI to accelerate research and analysis while keeping business judgment and final decisions with the responsible people.

Aha moment

My biggest realization was that a promising product idea is not the same as a validated business opportunity.

Instead of asking, "How can we launch an economical instant water heater?", I learned to ask, "What must we prove before we invest in scaling this product?"

This changed my approach from focusing primarily on launching the product to building evidence that customers will buy it at the target price and that the business can deliver it profitably. The 90-day validation phase became a way to reduce uncertainty before making a larger commitment.
